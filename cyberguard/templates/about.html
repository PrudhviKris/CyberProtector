About CyberGuard

What is cyberbullying?

Cyberbullying is the use of electronic communication to bully a person, typically by sending intimidating, insulting or threatening messages. It has become increasingly common, especially among teenagers, and it can leave long-lasting effects on mental health, social life and well-being.

Why detection matters

Detecting cyberbullying early can prevent further harm. Social media, messaging platforms and forums carry far more messages than human moderators can read, so automated systems that flag harmful content are essential for a safer online environment.

How the system works

CyberGuard uses Natural Language Processing and Machine Learning. Incoming text is normalised: lowercased, stripped of URLs and mentions, and decoded from obfuscations such as f*ck or $tupid. Two models then score it:

  • Classical model: TF-IDF features (word 1–2-grams and character 3–5-grams) fed to a tuned Logistic Regression classifier, trained on our labelled dataset.
  • Transformer model: unitary/toxic-bert, a BERT model fine-tuned on the Jigsaw Toxic Comment dataset, which scores six abuse categories.

The scores are blended into one bullying probability, graded by severity, and explained by highlighting the words that drove the decision.

Features

  • Instant analysis with category breakdown and explanations
  • Batch analysis of CSV/TXT files with downloadable results
  • Personal history for signed-in users
  • Admin analytics dashboard and help-desk ticket management
  • REST API with per-user API keys for integration into other apps

Mission

Our mission is to foster a safer online space where users feel respected and protected, using technology to reduce online harassment and support digital well-being, especially for young people, who are most vulnerable to online abuse.

Limitations

No model is perfect. Sarcasm, reclaimed slurs, code-mixed language and new slang can cause errors. CyberGuard is designed to assist human moderators, not replace them.
